What is PMOS?

The most common hormonal condition, and it affects the whole body

PMOS (polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome) is the new medical term for PCOS. The old name pointed only at the ovaries; the real condition is whole-body endocrine and metabolic dysregulation.

The clinical shift

Why the ovaries were always a distraction

For decades, medicine treated polycystic ovaries as an isolated gynaecological issue. In reality, the ovaries are innocent responders—caught in the crossfire of disrupted neuroendocrine signalling, hypothalamic feedback loops, and chronic hyperinsulinemia.

Rotterdam criteria without the wait

Diagnosis requires two of three clinical features: irregular ovulation, androgen excess, or polycystic morphology. Because ultrasound alone frequently misses lean Asian presentations, pattern synthesis across multiple cycles is essential.

Lifelong and managed, not cured

Ovulatory axis

Cycle irregularity

Delayed follicular maturation, luteal phase progesterone drops, missed or prolonged cycles, and cyclical mood and energy dips.

Androgenic axis

Physical signs

Follicular sensitivity to free testosterone leading to persistent cystic acne along the jawline, hirsutism, and crown hair thinning.

Metabolic axis

4.4x Asian risk

Impaired cellular glucose uptake and compensatory insulin surges, driving rapid energy crashes, stubborn weight gain, and long-term diabetes vulnerability.

The Asian gap

Four reasons diagnosis takes years in Singapore

Universal clinical thresholds were built on Western Caucasian cohorts. Asian women present differently and are routinely told their numbers are "normal".

Thresholds built on Western women

Asian women are frequently lean but insulin-resistant, with milder body hair. Standard cut-offs tell them they're fine, delaying care until diabetes or fertility struggles arise.

No single biomarker test

Diagnosis requires two of three features over time. Biomarker research was never adequately funded: PMOS received only $215M from the NIH over a decade, vs $454M for rheumatoid arthritis.

Symptoms arrive at different doors

Acne to dermatology, periods to gynae, weight to the GP, mood to therapy. Each specialist treats an isolated symptom; the contraceptive pill often masks the rest.

Care starts only at the crisis

In Singapore, dedicated PMOS clinics sit inside KKH's IVF fertility centre. Healthier SG assigns family doctors only from age 40. Women aged 20 to 35 have no anchor.

"Hormones affect the whole body. Healthcare treats one organ, one visit, one at a time. Nobody is paid to own the whole picture, so she becomes her own coordinator, with the least information to do it."
The root cause of fragmented care

Dietary reality in Southeast Asia

Western PCOS plans demand strict low-carb diets impossible to maintain in Singapore. Auvira's clinical dietitians build strategies calibrated for local hawker dining, white rice swaps, and insulin-balancing meals you can actually stick to.
